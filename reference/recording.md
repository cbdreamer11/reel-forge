# How to record so the editor can do its job

*[Versión en español](grabacion.md)*

None of this is required — the pipeline runs on whatever you give it. But these
habits cost nothing on set and change the result a lot, because they line up with
how take selection actually works.

---

## 1. Don't stop the recording. Just do the take again.

This is the one that matters most.

**The selector compares takes inside a single file.** Four attempts in one recording
means it sees all four, scores them, and keeps the best one on its own. Four separate
files means it never sees them together — nothing to compare, nothing to choose, and
you end up picking by hand.

So when a line comes out wrong: don't stop, don't restart the camera. **Pause, breathe,
say the whole thing again.** Keep rolling.

> One recording with four takes beats four recordings with one take each — every time.

Fewer files is better for the boring reasons too: less syncing, less transcription,
less admin.

## 2. Leave a full beat of silence between takes

About a second.

Cuts are found at silence — roughly 0.4s below −30dB. Run two attempts together
without breathing and they become a single block, and nothing downstream can separate
them again. The silence is the edit point. Give it to the machine.

## 3. Say the hook on its own, and say it first

The hook is the most commonly lost piece of a short, for a boring reason: people
start talking before they have settled, so the opening lands before the recording
really begins.

Say it deliberately, as its own take, with silence after it.

## 4. Repeat the closing three or four times on purpose

This is not wasted effort. The pipeline expects the closing to arrive in several
attempts and keeps the last one — that is the only place where repeating yourself
actively helps.

Say it, pause, say it better, pause, say it better again.

## 5. One idea per take. Never cross a pause mid-idea

If you flub a word halfway through a sentence, **go back and say the whole sentence
again** — not just the word.

The selector picks whole takes and never splices across them, because splicing shifts
the room tone and the breath and the viewer feels it without being able to name it. A
half-sentence that only works when glued to another half-sentence cannot be used by
anything.

## 6. A false start costs nothing

"Sorry, again" becomes its own little block and gets dropped automatically. You do not
need to be clean — you need to be complete.

## 7. Practical setup notes

- **Separate mic:** start the recorder before the camera and stop it after. Sync is
  automatic by cross-correlation, but it needs overlap to work with.
- **Framing:** leave some room above your head. The zoom anchors on the upper third,
  so a tight-to-the-top frame has nothing to move into.
- **Filenames:** name them however you like. If you keep a content list, `match`
  identifies each clip by listening to it.

---

## The short version

> Keep rolling. Silence between takes. Hook first, on its own. Closing three or four
> times. Whole sentences, never halves.
